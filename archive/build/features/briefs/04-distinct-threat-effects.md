# Distinct Threat Effects

## Purpose

Create mission variety by making a threat's category meaningful to play, not merely decorative.

## Expected behavior

1. Each cyber-threat category has a recognizable gameplay effect or behavior.
2. A mission can combine threat categories so its threat mix changes the sweep experience.
3. Threat-related behavior is communicated through in-game visual feedback and, where applicable, status or warning feedback.
4. The effect remains associated with its category consistently during play.

## Inputs / outputs

- **Inputs:** the threat categories included in a mission and the player's in-mission actions.
- **Outputs:** category-specific gameplay consequences or reactions that affect the ongoing mission experience.

## User-visible behavior

The player can perceive that threats behave differently and that mixed missions require attention to the kind of threat present. Effects reinforce the live-system feeling of the grid reacting to player decisions.

## Constraints

- Every cyber-threat category has its own recognizable effect or behavior.
- Effects must work within short, understandable missions.
- Effects must not obscure the player's ability to understand the board or outcome.

## Basic acceptance expectations

- Each included category produces a distinguishable gameplay effect or behavior.
- A mission with different threat mixes feels meaningfully different to play.
- The player receives feedback sufficient to connect an observed effect to a threat category.
